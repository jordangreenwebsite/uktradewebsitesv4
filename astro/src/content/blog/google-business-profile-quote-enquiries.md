A customer who finds your business on Google Maps may be comparing tradespeople, checking opening hours or trying to establish whether you cover their postcode. Your Business Profile can help them decide whether to get in touch, but only if the next step is straightforward.

## Start with accurate business details

Check that the name, telephone number, categories, service areas and opening hours are correct. If you work from home and do not receive customers there, follow Google's guidance for service-area businesses rather than advertising an address as a public shop.

Google's [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) explains the importance of accurate information and factors such as distance and relevance. Do not add towns to your business name just to try to improve your position.

## Send visitors to a page that answers their question

A customer arriving from Maps needs to understand quickly what you offer, where you work and how to contact you.

A roofer can make emergency roof repairs easy to find. A window cleaner might explain whether they take regular domestic customers or commercial contracts. A driveway cleaner can show different surface types and explain what is included in a quotation.

Give each service a sensible place in the navigation rather than making visitors look through unrelated jobs.

## Use photographs that help customers decide

Add genuine examples of completed work, equipment and relevant services. On the website, explain what the photograph shows. A before-and-after picture is useful if both views genuinely represent the same job.

Never use stock photographs as evidence of projects you completed. Avoid showing identifiable customer details without permission. Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews rather than offering rewards for positive feedback.

## Make the quote route easy on a phone

Open your profile on a mobile and follow the website link. Is the main telephone number clickable? Can you request a quote without hunting through menus?

A simple form usually needs a name, contact details, postcode and a brief description. Optional photographs may help some trades, but do not force people to complete a lengthy survey. Test the actual form delivery and confirmation message.

## Measure what happens after the profile visit

Business Profile performance can show interactions such as website clicks and calls where available. Use website analytics and Search Console for additional context. A click is not automatically a lead.

If you add tracking parameters to the website link, check it still opens the correct page and that analytics records the visit as intended. Keep a simple record of genuine enquiries and ask customers how they found you.

If people frequently ask whether you cover their town or offer a particular service, make that information easier to find.

## Give your profile and website the same job

The aim is not simply more profile visits. It is to help suitable customers understand the service and make a useful enquiry.
