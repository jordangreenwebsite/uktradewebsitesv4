If someone has been recommended your business, or has found you through a local search, your website should answer a few questions quickly: **What do you do? Do you cover my area? Can I trust you? How do I ask for a quote?**

You do not need dozens of pages to answer those questions. You need the right information arranged so that people can find it, especially when they are using a phone between jobs or while dealing with an urgent problem at home.

## Start with the essentials

A useful homepage normally makes five things clear:

1. **Your trade and main services.** Say what the business does in everyday language. “Roof repairs and replacements” is clearer than “complete property solutions”.
2. **The areas you genuinely cover.** Name your main town or region and link to a fuller service-area page where appropriate. Do not list places you would not travel to.
3. **A quick way to contact you.** Make calling, emailing or requesting a quote obvious, especially on mobile.
4. **Real evidence of your work.** A few well-chosen project photographs with explanations beat a gallery of unrelated stock photos.
5. **The next step.** Explain whether customers should send photos, ask for a visit, phone for an emergency or complete a short form.

An effective website also works with slow connections, displays well on small screens and does not bury contact information underneath complicated navigation.

## Give your services enough detail

A single paragraph saying you cover “all aspects” of your trade tells visitors very little. If you offer different kinds of work, consider giving each important service its own section or page.

For example, a roofing company may need to distinguish a leaking roof, flat-roof installation and replacement fascias. A driveway cleaner may need separate explanations for block paving, patio cleaning and re-sanding.

For each service, write about the work you actually do, what information helps you quote and the kinds of jobs you accept. Avoid copying one paragraph and simply swapping the service name.

## Make the proof genuine

Photographs, customer feedback and credentials can all help someone decide whether to make contact. They must be accurate.

- Use your own project photos wherever possible, with permission where needed.
- Describe what was completed rather than claiming every photograph is a dramatic transformation.
- Publish genuine customer testimonials only when you have permission and can stand behind them.
- Display relevant qualifications or professional memberships only when the business holds them.

If you are building a new business and do not have a large gallery yet, say so. Clear information and a professional, honest presentation are better than invented examples.

## Make enquiries easy to handle

Consider what you need to know before giving an estimate. A cleaning company might want a postcode, type of surface, approximate size and a photograph. A decorator may need room dimensions and a description of the finish.

Collecting a few relevant details can help, but a form that asks twenty questions will create unnecessary work for a potential customer. Give people a simpler way to call or email if they prefer.

Check the finished form on a real phone. Make sure the enquiry arrives at the correct address and that you can actually reply to it.

## What about search engines?

A clear page title, useful service descriptions, accessible headings and relevant internal links help search engines understand the site. They also make it more useful for visitors.

A new website is not a guarantee of rankings. Your Google Business Profile, competition, reputation and the quality of the pages all matter. Focus first on creating a website that accurately represents the business.

## A simple starting point

For many small trade businesses, a homepage, services, about, examples of work and contact information are enough to launch a useful first version. Additional service or location pages should earn their place by answering a real question.
