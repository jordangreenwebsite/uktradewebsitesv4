When planning a tradesperson's website, a common question is whether to build a page for every service, every town, or both.

There is no magic number of pages that guarantees Google visibility. The useful question is: **Will this page tell a potential customer something important they could not easily learn elsewhere on the site?**

## What is a service page?

A service page explains one type of work that your business provides.

For a plumber, that could be leak repairs or bathroom installation. For a landscaper, it might be patio construction or garden fencing. For a pressure-washing business, it could be [driveway cleaning](/websites-for-driveway-cleaners/) or commercial exterior cleaning.

A worthwhile service page can explain:

- What the job involves and which properties or surfaces you work on.
- What is included, and what is not.
- Genuine photographs or examples relevant to that service.
- Common questions that affect the quote.
- Which areas the service is available in.
- How the customer can contact you.

If two services are essentially the same for your business, it may be clearer to keep them together rather than create weak duplicate pages.

## What is an area page?

An area page helps customers understand the work you provide in a particular town or region.

It can be useful when you genuinely serve several distinct places. A roofer working in Swindon and Cirencester, for example, may have different examples of nearby jobs, local travel arrangements or service information worth showing to people in each place.

Useful area pages feature **actual local relevance**. They do not need to pretend you have an office in every town.

If the only thing that changes between pages is a town name in the heading, consider whether one clear “Areas We Cover” page would serve visitors better.

## Should you combine services and locations?

Sometimes. A business with a substantial volume of a specific service in a particular place may justify a page for that combination, provided it has distinct, useful content.

But automatically multiplying ten services by twenty towns can create hundreds of nearly identical pages. That is not a sound content plan.

Start with the important services and the places where you actually work. Build more pages as you collect genuine examples, customer questions and evidence that a page would help.

## Create a website structure people can follow

A sensible small-business structure might look like this:

- **Home:** the business, main services, coverage and contact route.
- **Services:** a clear overview with links to important individual services.
- **Areas covered:** your genuine service locations, with useful local detail where available.
- **Our work:** selected examples with honest descriptions.
- **About and contact:** who customers are dealing with, and how to reach you.

Link between related pages naturally. A patio-cleaning article might link to the [pressure washing website design](/websites-for-pressure-washers/) page if that helps explain the wider services, but avoid adding irrelevant links merely for SEO.

## Check what people already need

Before adding a new page, look at the questions customers regularly ask, which services bring profitable work, and where enquiries come from.

If the business already has a website, Google Search Console can also help identify which search queries lead people to your pages. Use that information to improve the pages customers are already finding, not just to publish more pages.

## The best rule

**Build pages for real people and real jobs, not imaginary search-engine opportunities.** A smaller website with specific, accurate information is a more useful starting point than a large collection of interchangeable location pages.
