More website traffic is not always the first thing a trade business needs. If people already visit your site but rarely get in touch, check whether they can quickly understand the service and take the next step.

A low number of enquiries can have several explanations. Some visitors may be outside your service area, comparing options, or looking for something you do not offer. Others may be interested but unable to find your contact details.

Here is a practical way to investigate without guessing.

## 1. Check the site on your own phone

Open the website as if you were a customer. Without scrolling too far, can you see:

- What trade the business works in?
- The main service you came looking for?
- Where the business operates?
- A telephone number or clear quote button?

Try tapping the navigation and contact buttons. Check that text is readable and no part of the page moves off-screen.

A form that looks fine on a desktop may be difficult to complete on a small phone, especially if the fields are tiny or the keyboard covers the submit button.

## 2. Make your services specific

“Quality work at competitive prices” could describe thousands of companies.

Tell people which jobs you accept. A roofer might say “flat roof repairs, pitched-roof repairs and new roof installations”. A cleaner might show separate driveway, patio and gutter services. This helps people decide if you are relevant to their problem.

Avoid making visitors phone simply to find out whether you do a particular kind of work.

## 3. Give customers a reason to trust you

People often want evidence before inviting a tradesperson to their property.

Use genuine photographs of your work with short explanations. Include accurate service areas, real company details and relevant credentials. Genuine testimonials can help, but there is no need to exaggerate numbers or invent reviews.

Where you use stock imagery for visual presentation, avoid suggesting it shows jobs your business completed.

## 4. Test every enquiry route

Send a test message through your contact form. Does it arrive in the right inbox? Are the details legible and complete? Does the thank-you page work?

Tap the phone link on a mobile, open the email link and check any WhatsApp button. A stylish website cannot generate useful leads if the buttons do not work.

If forms are long, ask yourself which fields you genuinely need for an initial conversation. Name, contact method, postcode and a brief description are often enough to begin.

## 5. Look at where visitors actually land

Not everyone begins on your homepage. Someone searching for a specific service may open a service page directly.

Each important page should make sense on its own: say what is offered, show relevant proof and include a route to enquire.

Link customers to related information when useful. For example, a driveway-cleaning page may naturally lead to patio cleaning or re-sanding, without listing every unrelated service on the same screen.

## 6. Measure before redesigning everything

If you use analytics, look for the pages people visit, the devices they use and whether enquiry events are recorded correctly. Google Search Console can help you understand which queries bring visits from Google Search.

Numbers alone do not explain why somebody did not enquire. Use them alongside real tests and customer feedback. Before buying more traffic, repair obvious issues and assess whether visitors are actually looking for your service.

## Start with the most important fix

You may not need a complete redesign. Making the contact button obvious, explaining a confusing service or correcting a broken form can be a sensible first move.
