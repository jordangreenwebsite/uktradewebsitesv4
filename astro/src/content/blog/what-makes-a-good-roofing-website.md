A roofing website does not need to be complicated, but it must answer the right questions. A visitor with water coming through a ceiling is looking for something different from somebody planning a roof replacement next year.

A good site explains what work you undertake, where you work, why someone can trust you and how to request a quote without having to search for the contact details.

## Make the main roofing services obvious

Avoid a homepage that says only “all aspects of roofing”. Name the services you genuinely offer. A roofer might have dedicated pages for:

- Roof leak investigation and repairs.
- Pitched roof repairs and replacement.
- Flat roofs on garages and extensions.
- Fascias, soffits and guttering.
- Storm-related roof damage, where that service is actually offered.

Each page should say what sort of problem you can look at and how the quote process works. Do not create separate pages just to repeat the same words with a different town name.

If you want more detailed advice about service pages, read [what a trade business website should include](/blog/what-should-a-trade-business-website-include/).

## Use photographs from your real jobs

A gallery can make a difference, but only if visitors know what they are looking at. A photograph of a roof from the pavement needs a caption: was it a replacement, a repair or a guttering job? A before-and-after pair should genuinely show the same work.

Ask permission to publish photographs of identifiable properties. Avoid displaying a customer's house number or other details that are not necessary. Stock photographs are fine as illustration, but they are not evidence of jobs your company completed.

## Explain the places you cover honestly

A genuine service-area statement is more useful than dozens of identical local pages. If you cover Swindon and nearby villages, say so and explain any limits or callout arrangements. Do not imply you have offices in towns where you do not.

Make your business details consistent with your Google Business Profile. Google's [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) is a sensible reference when reviewing coverage and listing information.

## Give people confidence without overpromising

Show the company name, working contact details and genuine reviews. You can include qualifications or trade memberships when they are current and verifiable. Do not add badges simply because competitors display them.

If pricing depends on scaffolding, materials, access and roof condition, explain that rather than inventing a fixed price. A homeowner should understand when an inspection may be needed before you can give an accurate quote.

## Make contacting you simple on a phone

Put a prominent phone link in the header and a clear “Request a quote” action on service pages. A short form can ask for the postcode, type of roof, the problem and contact details. Optional photographs can make the first conversation easier.

Then test the actual form. Submit a realistic enquiry on a mobile, confirm it arrives in the correct inbox and check the confirmation shown to the customer.

Do not label a contact button “emergency callout” unless you actually offer that service.

## Keep the website technically sound

Each service page needs a clear heading, a descriptive search title and links to the next useful page. Images should load efficiently and have honest descriptions. The site should work on smaller screens and avoid menus that hide the contact button.

Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is a useful foundation. Good structure helps people find information, but no web designer can promise you a particular organic position.

## A useful roofing website starts with clarity

If people can identify the right service, see credible examples, understand your coverage and contact you easily, the site is doing the important jobs.
